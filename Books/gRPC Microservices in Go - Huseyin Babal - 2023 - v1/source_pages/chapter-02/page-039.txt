2.4
Service discovery
Service discovery is the operation in which service locations are managed and exposed
outside to let each service find the next one for step execution. There are two types of
service discovery:
Client-side service discovery—In this notation, a service discovery tool allows appli-
cations to report their locations during startup, as shown in figure 2.6. Client
applications have direct connections to the service registry, and they query the
location of a specific service by providing some criteria, like the service name,
or a unique identifier.
Server-side service discovery—A load balancer integrates with the service registry to
resolve downstream services. Client applications connect to services via the load
balancer instead of using the service registry to resolve the exact location, as
visualized in figure 2.7.
Apart from those two discovery mechanisms, container orchestration platforms such
as Kubernetes have built-in service discovery mechanisms that allow you to access any
service by its name, which we will see in detail in chapter 8. Now that we understand
service discovery, which enables services to communicate with each other, let’s see
how gRPC comes into play.

The two data structures have the same spatial locality, so we may expect a similar exe-
cution time for these two functions. But the function iterating on the slice is signifi-
cantly faster (about 70% on my machine). What’s the reason?
 To understand this, we have to discuss the concept of striding. Striding relates to
how CPUs work through data. There are three different types of strides (see figure
12.6):
Unit stride—All the values we want to access are allocated contiguously: for
example, a slice of int64 elements. This stride is predictable for a CPU and the
most efficient because it requires a minimum number of cache lines to walk
through the elements.
Constant stride—Still predictable for the CPU: for example, a slice that iterates
over every two elements. This stride requires more cache lines to walk through
data, so it’s less efficient than a unit stride.

Understanding the fundamental differences between heap and stack should
also be part of your core knowledge when optimizing a Go application. Stack
allocations are almost free, whereas heap allocations are slower and rely on the
GC to clean the memory.
Reducing allocations is also an essential aspect of optimizing a Go application.
This can be done in different ways, such as designing the API carefully to pre-
vent sharing up, understanding the common Go compiler optimizations, and
using sync.Pool.
Use the fast-path inlining technique to efficiently reduce the amortized time to
call a function.
Rely on profiling and the execution tracer to understand how an application
performs and the parts to optimize.
Understanding how to tune the GC can lead to multiple benefits such as han-
dling sudden load increases more efficiently.
To help avoid CPU throttling when deployed in Docker and Kubernetes, keep
in mind that Go isn’t CFS-aware.
